## [Cool URIs Should Not Change](https://www.w3.org/Provider/Style/URI) ⭐️ 8.0/10

The 1998 W3C essay “Cool URIs Don’t Change” argues that well-designed URLs should remain stable indefinitely and should not be altered without necessity. Its core guidance continues to apply to web architecture, documentation, interoperability, and digital preservation. Stable URLs preserve links between websites, software, documentation, and historical records, reducing link rot and maintaining access to information. The principle remains important despite modern redirect systems because neglect, reorganization, and site shutdowns can still make old resources unavailable. The essay emphasizes choosing durable URL structures and avoiding unnecessary dependence on changing filenames, organizational structures, or technologies. Community examples show that 301 and 302 redirects can mitigate URL changes, but they do not eliminate failures such as generic landing pages, neglected redirects, or 404 errors.

hackernews · Klaster\_1 · Aug 9, 14:32 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49231809)

**Background**: A URI is an identifier used to refer to a resource on the web, and a URL is a common kind of URI that also indicates where the resource can be accessed. Link rot occurs when a hyperlink no longer reaches its original resource because the resource was moved, removed, or became unavailable. Persistent URIs help ensure that references remain usable even as the underlying content or web infrastructure changes.
