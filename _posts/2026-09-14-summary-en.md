## [Why Shorter HBM Stacks May Win in AI Accelerators](https://newsletter.semianalysis.com/p/long-live-the-short-king-why-4-hi) ⭐️ 8.0/10

The article argues that AI accelerator memory design is shifting from ever-denser HBM stacks toward shorter configurations: 8-hi may replace 12-hi as the standard, while 4-hi could become attractive for inference. Nvidia’s Rubin Ultra is cited as using 192GB of HBM per GPU, down from 288GB in standard Rubin, largely because scarce HBM wafers cannot support the expected volume of 12-hi stacks. Shorter stacks could produce more HBM cubes from limited DRAM wafer capacity, helping alleviate supply constraints and control accelerator costs. For inference, where bandwidth often matters more than maximum capacity, 4-hi HBM may reduce cost per token when additional capacity delivers diminishing returns. HBM stacks vertically combine multiple DRAM dies and connect them with Through-Silicon Vias, while packaging them close to the accelerator on a silicon interposer. The article says Rubin Ultra’s reduction reflects both wafer scarcity and architectural changes, including a shift from an earlier expectation of four compute dies to two, so its per-die capacity falls from 256GB to 96GB.

rss · SemiAnalysis · Sep 13, 18:19

**Background**: HBM is a 3D-stacked memory technology designed to provide much higher bandwidth than conventional DDR DRAM while offering more capacity than on-chip SRAM. A “4-hi” or “8-hi” designation describes the number of DRAM dies stacked vertically in one memory stack. HBM stacks are placed near the GPU or other accelerator and linked through advanced packaging, allowing wide, high-speed memory connections.

Intel Macs are classified as Tier 3, which means they are not officially supported and will no longer receive new precompiled packages, although compatibility may remain for some software. Landlock is designed to let unprivileged processes add restrictions on filesystem or network access as an additional security layer.
